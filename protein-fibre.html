7 practical options. Larger snacks can work as a small meal.

01EVERYDAY DEFAULT · NO COOK

Roasted chana
+ yogurt

40 g roasted chana + 150 g plain low-fat Greek yogurt

19–21 gprotein
5–7 gfibre

Eat separately, or add jeera and cucumber to the yogurt. Keep the chana crunchy until you eat.

Easy alternative

Use 200 g ordinary low-fat dahi if preferred. It usually supplies ~7–9 g protein, versus ~12 g in this 150 g Greek-yogurt serving, so the snack total will be lower.

02SWEET OPTION · NO COOK

Yogurt, guava
+ oats

200 g low-fat Greek yogurt + 100 g guava + 20 g oats

20–22 gprotein
7–8 gfibre

Mix, soften the oats in the yogurt, and add chopped guava. No honey or granola needed.

Easy alternative

Use an apple, pear or orange instead. Fibre changes with the fruit and portion. Ordinary dahi works too, but 200 g typically supplies ~7–9 g protein rather than ~16 g.

03BUSY-DAY DEFAULT · NO COOK

Whey
+ whole fruit

Whey supplying 24 g protein + one apple or orange

24–25 gprotein
3–5 gfibre

Use water for a simple shake. The fruit brings the fibre; the shake supplies most of the protein.

Make it fit your day

Count any whey already used at breakfast. Milk adds nutrients and calories. Whey is optional and can fill a food gap on rest days too; it is not reserved for workouts.

04LEFTOVER DEFAULT · COOKED BEANS

Kala chana
or chole chaat

150 g cooked beans + onion, tomato and lemon

12–14 gprotein
10–13 gfibre

Toss ready-cooked beans with vegetables, coriander and spices. No cooking today if the beans are already prepared.

Add protein or change the beans

Add 100 g Greek yogurt for ~8 g more protein. Lobia or cooked whole moong are useful swaps; their nutrient totals differ. Skip a large sev topping.

05WARM & SAVOURY · PAN COOK

Besan chillas
+ yogurt

2 chillas from 50 g besan + 100 g low-fat Greek yogurt

18–20 gprotein
5–7 gfibre

Add grated vegetables to the batter. Use about 1 tsp oil across the batch. This is a substantial snack or small meal.

Easy alternative

Make moong chilla or pesarattu instead; the dry-pulse portion determines the protein. See the meal swaps below for a breakfast-sized serving.

06DAIRY-FREE · BOIL & TOSS

Masala soy
chunk chaat

30 g dry soy chunks + chopped vegetables

15–17 gprotein
4–6 gfibre*

Boil following the packet, squeeze, then toss with vegetables, lemon and chaat spices.

Check the portion

Weigh the chunks dry: soaking adds water, not protein. Fibre varies substantially by brand, so check the pack. This serving may supply less than 5 g fibre.

07LIGHT & SIMPLE · COOK EGGS

Egg whites
+ fruit

4 cooked egg whites + one apple or orange

14–15 gprotein
3–5 gfibre

Boil or scramble the whites; enjoy the fruit alongside. Useful when you do not want dairy or pulses.

Easy alternative

For breakfast, use one whole egg plus extra whites and wholegrain toast. Count the yolk and bread in the meal totals; this snack estimate is for four whites and fruit only.

Protein without extra saturated fat: favour soy, pulses, lower-fat dairy, fish and skinless chicken over simply adding more regular paneer. Reducing saturated fat and including soluble fibre support LDL lowering. Why this helps ↗

02 / KEEP THE HOUSEHOLD MEAL

One useful upgrade.

No separate menu needed. Keep the family dish and strengthen your own serving.

POHA / VEGETABLE UPMA

Add soy granules.

Cook 30 g dry soy granules into your portion, following the packet. Adds roughly 15 g protein.

Alternative: eggs or a protein-rich yogurt side.

MOONG CHILLA / PESARATTU

Measure before soaking.

Use 60 g dry moong per person: roughly 13–15 g protein. Add yogurt or tofu filling to move nearer 25 g.

Alternative: besan chilla; pair it with a protein side.

DAL + ROTI + SABZI

Add a protein side.

Serve 100–150 g tofu or protein-rich yogurt alongside. A small bowl of thin dal usually needs help to reach the meal target.

Portion tip: count lentils in the batch and divide by servings. Water changes bowl size, not protein.

RAJMA / CHOLE / LOBIA / WHOLE MOONG

Serve beans generously.

Aim for 150–180 g cooked beans, with vegetables and rice or roti. Add yogurt, tofu or eggs if the meal needs more protein.

Alternative: rotate kala chana, matki and masoor; try sundal, usal or cooked-moong chaat.

KHICHDI

Make it more dal-rich.

Try equal dry weights of dal and rice, plus vegetables. Add soy granules or a yogurt side for extra protein.

Portion tip: keep your usual total grain-and-dal amount to start; change the ratio.

PALAK / METHI PANEER / BHURJI

Swap the paneer.

Use tofu or genuinely lower-fat paneer. Keep the familiar spices and vegetable base.

Label tip: tofu protein varies. “High protein” paneer can still be high in saturated fat.

CHICKEN / ROHU CURRY

Add the fibre alongside.

Use 100–120 g cooked edible chicken or 120–150 g cooked edible fish as a starting portion. Add sabzi and whole grains or pulses.

Portion tip: bones and gravy do not count as meat. Skinless curry cuts work; no air fryer required.

ORDINARY DAHI IS FINE TOO

Same bowl. Different protein.

7–9 g200 g ordinary dahi

~16 g200 g Greek yogurt*

Use the affordable option you enjoy. If switching to dahi, fill the protein difference elsewhere.

*Assumes 8 g protein per 100 g. Choose plain, lower-fat versions and check the label.

TOTAL FIBRE ≠ SOLUBLE FIBRE

Give soluble fibre a place.

The main guide aims towards 5–10 g soluble fibre within the daily total. Include oats or barley/jau, beans/lentils and fruit regularly.

A food with 5 g total fibre does not necessarily contain 5 g soluble fibre. NHLBI fibre guide ↗

BUILD UP COMFORTABLY

Let your gut adjust.

Increase portions gradually if you currently eat much less fibre. Spread pulses across meals, soak and cook them thoroughly, and drink regularly.

If you feel bloated, slow the increase and use smaller portions. You do not need to force a large chana bowl on top of an already fibre-rich day.

05 / KNOW WHAT A FOOD DOES WELL

Useful food. Different jobs.

Makhana, murmura
and plain popcorn

Good for crunch; modest protein. Pair with yogurt, roasted chana or another protein source.

Nuts and
peanut butter

Useful for taste and fats. Keep a modest portion; they are not an efficient way to close a 20 g protein gap.

Sattu
drinks

30 g sattu usually gives ~6–7 g protein. Convenient, but not equivalent to a whey serving.

Sprouts
and portions

Water makes the bowl bigger. Do not count sprouted weight as dry-pulse weight. Cook sprouts before eating.

A little prep.
Many easy choices.

  1. Ask the cook for one ready pulse.Cook chana, lobia or moong. Set aside a portion before gravy for chaat; refrigerate promptly and freeze portions for later days.
  2. Keep two easy protein options.Plain lower-fat yogurt plus tofu, soy chunks, eggs or whey. Add soy/tofu to a few vegetarian meals.

Portions, sources and how to use this guide

All nutrient amounts are approximate planning estimates, not lab-tested recipes. Use your current pack for yogurt, soy, whey and other packaged foods. A cup is about 240 mL; a katori is not a standard measure. Recipe oil, gravy and toppings also contribute to daily calories and saturated fat.

These starting targets accompany the main guide’s adjustable energy template. There is no need to hit every number exactly each day. This page does not promise a particular cholesterol response or replace individual clinical advice.
